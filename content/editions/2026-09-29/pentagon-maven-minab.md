Bloomberg reports on a Pentagon review of the February 28 Tomahawk strike on the Shajarah Tayyebeh Elementary School in Minab, Iran, which killed at least 123 children. The school was still listed as an IRGC base, although it had been a school since around 2017-18, and an analyst's 2019 note about the change never reached strike planners.

The review calls the outcome "a cascade of preventable failures." Among them: CENTCOM used Palantir's Maven Smart System, which compressed hours of target work into minutes, and users expected it to flag stale intelligence, which it was not designed to do. Civilian-harm staff had been cut by about 90%, and the opening assault covered more than 1,000 targets in 24 hours, according to reporting summarised by Gizmodo.

In a [follow-up](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-22/us-military-modifies-ai-combat-targeting-after-iran-minab-school-strike) on September 22, Bloomberg reported the military has reworked how targets are refreshed and vetted, added open-source feeds on civilian movement and made dozens of changes to Maven, including features to re-review underlying intelligence and flag inconsistencies. The review itself has not been released.
