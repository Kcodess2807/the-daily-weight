On September 25 the D.C. Circuit ruled 2-1 to uphold Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth's March designation of Anthropic as a supply-chain risk, which bars the military from using Anthropic's models and defence contractors from using them on Pentagon work. The designation followed Anthropic's refusal to remove restrictions on autonomous weapons and mass surveillance.

Writing for the majority, Judge Gregory Katsas found the Pentagon had enough evidence that Claude's restrictions and the unresolved contract dispute could make it unreliable, and rejected Anthropic's free-speech and due-process claims, according to Reuters. Judge Karen LeCraft Henderson dissented, arguing the law does not treat "a contractor's honest and upfront enforcement of restrictions" as a supply-chain risk.

Anthropic said it "respectfully disagrees" and may seek a rehearing by the full court. A separate federal judge in San Francisco last month struck down a parallel government-wide designation under a different statute as unlawful retaliation.
