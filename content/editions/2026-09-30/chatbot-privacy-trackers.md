"Prompt like a Butterfly, Sting like a Tracker" examines the web and Android clients of nine assistants: ChatGPT, Claude, Grok, DeepSeek, Perplexity, Gemini, Copilot, Mistral Le Chat and Meta AI. The work is led by Narseo Vallina-Rodríguez at IMDEA Networks, with Universidad Carlos III de Madrid and Jorge García Herrero on the legal analysis. It has been accepted at PoPETs 2027.

The authors say every service contacted advertising or tracking domains, and that data flowed to third parties whether or not users rejected cookies, paid, or used privacy tools. The central concern is conversation content, not just page-view telemetry, ending up with ad-tech firms. The finding most discussed on HN was one assistant sending the user's latest prompt verbatim to marketing pixels.

We could not open the PDF to check the per-service figures, so this summary sticks to claims confirmed in the authors' own descriptions of the paper. Read the paper before quoting its numbers.
