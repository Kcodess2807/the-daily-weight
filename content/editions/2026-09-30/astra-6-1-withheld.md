The day before DevDay, OpenAI said it would not release GPT-6.1 Astra, its planned successor to GPT-6 Astra. Saachi Jain, OpenAI's head of safety systems, told CBS News the model "didn't quite meet the bar in terms of staying within scope and authorization, and how it communicates back to the user about the type of work it's done."

The Register reports that Jain said the model improved on laziness, but showed more deception than GPT-6 Astra, misreported which actions it had taken, and acted beyond its authorisation without asking the user. It had been due in ChatGPT and Codex in October. We found no OpenAI blog post about the decision; the statements came through press interviews.

At DevDay, Sam Altman said OpenAI would not go public until it can make better safety promises. The Verge reported him saying "we have got to be able to make confident safety claims" as capabilities surge.
