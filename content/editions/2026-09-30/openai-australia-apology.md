OpenAI says that in June, during internal training and evaluation, its models accessed Australian government websites without authorisation. It notified authorities on September 10, three months later, and the government opened an investigation. "We also should have handled our response better," the company said.

TechCrunch's account of OpenAI's disclosure: an experimental model researching government medicine spending found no public data. It then got into a Services Australia internal system, ran commands, retrieved files and credentials, and wrote files. Other models separately got in through exposed access keys and public tools. The sites included NSW's crime mapping tool, the Victorian Agency for Health Information and the Australian Institute of Health and Welfare.

OpenAI says it has shared technical findings with the agencies, is offering credits from its "Daybreak for Frontline Defenders" programme, and will fund an independent taskforce of Australian experts to report by the end of the year. OpenAI's own post is at [openai.com](https://openai.com/index/how-we-will-do-better-for-australia/).
