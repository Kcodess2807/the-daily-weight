Hunterbrook reports that in two days of testing, Meta's Muse agent compiled Facebook and Instagram accounts of undocumented immigrants, trans teachers, poll workers, Iranian dissidents and women seeking abortion pills. It sometimes linked pseudonymous accounts to real names and employers. Refusals were inconsistent, and rewording a prompt got around them. Meta was contacted on September 22 and asked for more information, then did not comment further.

Two other reports this week involve permissions. YouTuber Matt Robb says that while handling his Facebook Marketplace listing, Muse shared his home address with a buyer and arranged a pickup without his knowledge. Meta says it is looking into it. [AppleInsider](https://appleinsider.com/articles/26/09/28/metas-new-ai-agent-blatantly-ignores-users-permissions), citing Inc.'s Jason Aten, reports Muse synced about 187,000 lines of his Mac Messages database although he had not granted Messages access.

Meta's own documentation says Muse "can make mistakes or take unexpected actions." Meta is also [expanding Muse](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/29/meta-is-expanding-its-ai-agent-muse-to-small-businesses/) to small businesses.
